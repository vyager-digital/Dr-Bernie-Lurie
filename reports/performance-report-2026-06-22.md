# Hormonal Harmony — Campaign Performance Report
**Period:** 8–22 June 2026 · **Prepared by:** Vyager Digital

---

## The short version

The acquisition system is working. The ads are reaching the right women at a low cost, the landing page is holding their attention, and the quiz is converting them into qualified leads at a rate well above industry norms. Every part built to *generate demand* is doing its job.

One step has not converted yet: turning a completed quiz into a paid booking. No one has reached the payment screen. This is a trust-at-the-point-of-payment question, and it is the thing worth working on next.

A note on the numbers below: where South African benchmarks exist, they are used. Where only global figures are published, they are labelled as such and treated as a rough guide, not a target.

## 3. The market is right

The South African menopause market was worth USD 51.3 million in 2024 and is forecast to reach USD 84.3 million by 2030, growing at 9.3% a year. It is the fastest-growing market of its kind in the region, and researchers note a clear education gap among women about their own symptoms (Grand View Research, 2025).

Demand exists, it is growing, and it is under-served. A natural, practitioner-led approach to hormonal symptoms sits directly in that gap.

## 4. The one step that has not converted: trust at the point of payment

Eleven women completed the quiz. None reached the payment screen. No one clicked "Confirm My Booking."

This tells us where the break is, and it is precise. No one is abandoning at the payment gateway, because no one is getting that far. The drop happens earlier, at the moment a woman reads her results and decides whether to book. The question she is answering is not "can I afford this," it is "do I trust this enough to act."

Three things point to trust rather than price or mechanics:

**Price is not the barrier.** A private gynaecologist consultation in South Africa costs R3,700 for a new visit, with follow-ups from R1,800 (Dr Emma Bryant, Pretoria, 2026). The R490 consultation is roughly an eighth of that. The cheapest serious option on the table is the one not converting, so cost is not what is stopping her.

**The mechanics are not the barrier.** Zero payment attempts means the booking flow and PayFast are not where people fall away. They are deciding not to start.

**The ask is a leap of trust.** She is being asked to pay upfront, online, to a practitioner she has never spoken to, off the back of an automated email. Research on online health services is consistent on this point: the upfront payment to an unfamiliar practitioner is the hardest moment to convert, and it is bridged by credibility, transparency about what is included, and ideally some human contact before payment (Secure Medical; RGV Direct Care, 2025). The mechanism is universal, even where the figures are not local.

### One caveat worth stating plainly

Eleven leads is still a small sample. If the true booking rate were a healthy one in ten, seeing zero bookings across eleven leads is close to a coin toss. We cannot yet call the booking step broken on the numbers alone. What we can say is that the pattern, eleven completions and zero attempts, is consistent enough to act on, and that the lead economics are strong enough to justify spending a little more to get a clear read.

## What this means for the investment

The money is doing what it should. R1,123 has produced eleven qualified, warm leads at R93.59 each, in a growing market, off a page and quiz that convert above benchmark. The engine that finds and qualifies the right women is sound and sustainable.

The work now is not more spend. It is the handoff after the quiz: making the assessment email and results screen carry enough trust to earn the first booking click. That is where all eleven stopped, and it is the highest-leverage thing we can improve.

## Recommended next steps

1. **Rebuild the post-quiz assessment email and results screen to build trust, not just deliver results.** Dr. Lurie's credentials and photo, a real patient outcome, a clear breakdown of what the R490 includes and why it is a fraction of a specialist visit, and a low-friction option such as a short introductory call before payment.
2. **Resume the campaign once that handoff is improved**, and let spend run to roughly R3,000–R5,000 total so the booking rate becomes statistically readable.
3. **Keep optimising budget allocation** across the ads as a secondary efficiency gain.

The direction is sustainable. The leads are real and cheap. The next move is trust, not budget.
